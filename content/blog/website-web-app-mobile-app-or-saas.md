The short answer:

- A **website** tells people about your business and brings in enquiries.
- A **web app** lets people (customers or your team) **do work** in the browser: book, track, manage, pay.
- A **mobile app** does that from the phone's home screen, with notifications and offline features.
- A **SaaS product** is a web app you **sell to many businesses**, each with their own data.

Many businesses start by asking for "an app" when a website would do, or ask for "a website" when what they really need is a system to run their work. Here is how to tell which one you need, with examples from products I have built.

## When is a website enough?

A website is right when your main goal is to **be found and get contacted**: show what you offer, build trust, and collect enquiries.

Examples: the real estate websites I built for [Infinity Realty](https://infinityrealtypune.com/) and [LandCity Properties](https://landcityproperties.com/), or the site for [NVA Associates](https://nvaassociates.com/), an accounting and compliance firm. Visitors read, compare and enquire. Nobody needs to log in.

A website is the right start if:

- Customers mainly need information before they call or visit.
- Your sales happen offline, on the phone or in person.
- You want to show up on Google and AI assistants for what you offer.

## When do you need a web app?

You need a web app when people need to **log in and get something done**, and the work is easier on a computer or any device's browser.

Examples:

- **[Enquiro](/projects/enquiro)**, my enquiry management system: admins allocate leads, employees comment and update status, and managers export reports.
- **[Coworkspace](/projects/coworkspace)**: clients log in to book meeting rooms, and the admin team manages clients and emails invoices.
- Booking systems, dashboards, admin panels and internal tools that replace spreadsheets.

A web app is right if:

- Your team spends time copying data between Excel, email and WhatsApp.
- Customers need to book, track or pay for something themselves.
- Different people should see different data (admin, employee, client).

A website and a web app often go together: the website brings in the enquiry, and the web app manages it.

## When do you need a mobile app?

You need a mobile app when people will use it **often, on the move**, and benefit from things phones do well: push notifications, the camera, location, or working with a poor connection.

Example: the **Book An Artist** app I built with React Native, which is live on Google Play and the Apple App Store, lets clients and artists use the marketplace from their phones.

A mobile app is right if:

- Your users come back several times a week, not once.
- Notifications are central (new bookings, messages, reminders).
- People use it in the field, away from a desk.

If people will use it once or twice, a mobile-friendly web app is usually cheaper and easier, because nobody has to install anything. With React Native, one codebase can serve both Android and iOS when you do need an app.

## When are you building SaaS?

You are building SaaS when you want to **sell the same software to many businesses**, each paying for it and each seeing only their own data.

Example: at Aartas I built core parts of **HiGalen**, a multi-tenant healthcare SaaS platform used by 500+ doctors, with organization-level data separation and role-based access.

SaaS needs things a single-business web app does not:

- **Multi-tenancy**: each customer's data is kept separate.
- **Roles and permissions** inside each customer's account.
- **Sign-up, billing and subscriptions.**
- **Reliability**, because many businesses depend on it at once.

If you have built a tool for your own business and other businesses want it too, that is often how a SaaS product starts.

## How do you decide?

Ask yourself:

1. **Do people only need to learn about us and contact us?** Start with a website.
2. **Do people need to log in and do work?** You need a web app.
3. **Will they use it daily, on the move, with notifications?** Consider a mobile app.
4. **Do I want to sell this software to other businesses?** You are building SaaS.

You can also start small and grow: a website first, a web app when the process outgrows spreadsheets, and a mobile app or SaaS when the demand is proven.
