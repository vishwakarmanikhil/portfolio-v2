Most businesses don't lose leads because their website is bad. They lose them **after the enquiry arrives**: it sits in an inbox, gets buried in a WhatsApp group, or two salespeople each assume the other one called back.

The fix is not complicated. **Every enquiry needs one place to live, one person responsible for it, and a visible status** until it is closed. This post explains how to set that up, whether you use a spreadsheet or a proper system.

## Why do businesses lose website enquiries?

When I talk to business owners, especially in real estate, I hear the same problems again and again:

- **Enquiries go to one email inbox** that only one person checks.
- **Leads are forwarded to a WhatsApp group**, and nobody knows who has called whom.
- **The Excel sheet is out of date**, because updating it is a separate job nobody has time for.
- **Nobody knows the history.** When a customer calls back, the person who answers has no idea what was discussed before.
- **When an employee leaves**, their leads and notes leave with them.

None of these is a technology problem. They are ownership and visibility problems.

## What does a good enquiry process look like?

A process that works has four parts:

1. **One place for every enquiry.** Website forms, landing pages and other sources all end up in the same list.
2. **One owner for every enquiry.** Each enquiry is allocated to a specific person, so "I thought you called them" can't happen.
3. **A status and a history.** Every enquiry shows where it stands (new, contacted, site visit, closed) and has notes from each conversation.
4. **A manager's view.** The owner or manager can see all enquiries, filter them, and export a report at any time.

You can do this in a shared spreadsheet when you are very small. Once you have more than one or two people handling enquiries, a spreadsheet starts to break: people overwrite each other, forget to update it, and can see leads they shouldn't.

## What else helps you close more enquiries?

Once the basics are in place, these make a real difference:

- **Reply fast.** An enquiry is warmest in the first few minutes. Instant alerts by email or WhatsApp when a new enquiry arrives help a lot.
- **Set a next follow-up date** on every open enquiry, and check the list of follow-ups due today every morning.
- **Record why you lost a lead.** "Too expensive", "wrong location" and "bought elsewhere" tell you what to fix.
- **Look at the numbers monthly.** Which source brings the best enquiries? Which employee converts best? Where do leads get stuck?

## Should you build your own system or use an off-the-shelf CRM?

Large CRMs are powerful, but many small businesses find them expensive and complicated, and end up using a tenth of the features. A focused system that matches how your team already works is often a better fit, especially when it connects directly to your own website's forms.

Either way, choose something your team will actually use every day. A simple system that everyone uses beats a powerful one that nobody updates.
