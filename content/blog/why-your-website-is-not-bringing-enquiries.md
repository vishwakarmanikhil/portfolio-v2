A website that brings in no enquiries has one of two problems: **not enough of the right people are finding it**, or **the people who find it don't trust it enough, or can't act quickly enough, to get in touch**. The fix is completely different for each, so the first step is to find out which one you have.

This post walks through how to check, and the most common fixes I make on client websites.

## Is the problem traffic or conversion?

Before changing anything, look at two numbers for the last three months:

- **Visitors**: how many people visited the site (Google Analytics, or Google Search Console for visits from Google).
- **Enquiries**: how many forms, calls and WhatsApp messages came from the website.

Then:

- **Very few visitors?** You have a **traffic problem**. People can't enquire on a site they never see. Jump to [how to get found](#how-do-you-get-more-of-the-right-visitors).
- **Visitors, but almost no enquiries?** You have a **conversion problem**. People arrive and leave. Start with the next section.

If you have no analytics at all, add it first. Guessing wastes more money than anything else on this list.

## Does the first screen say what you do and who it's for?

Visitors decide in a few seconds whether they are in the right place. The first screen, before any scrolling, should answer:

- **What do you offer?** "Residential plots near Pune", not "Building dreams since 2005".
- **Who is it for?** First-time buyers, investors, small businesses, clinics.
- **What should I do next?** One clear button: "Enquire now", "Book a site visit", "Get a quote".

If your homepage opens with a slider of generic photos and a slogan, this is usually the quickest big win.

## Does the site load fast on a phone?

Most visitors arrive on a phone, often on mobile data. A site that takes several seconds to show anything loses people before they read a word.

Check it with Google's free [PageSpeed Insights](https://pagespeed.web.dev/) on the mobile setting. The usual causes of a slow site are:

- Huge images uploaded straight from a camera or phone.
- Sliders, videos and animations on the first screen.
- Too many plugins or third-party scripts (chat widgets, trackers, pop-ups).

Also open your own site on your phone and try to use it with one thumb. Buttons that are too small, text you have to zoom into, and menus that don't open properly all cost enquiries.

## Do visitors trust you enough to share their number?

Asking for a phone number is asking for trust. Give people reasons:

- **Real photos** of your work, your office, your team and your customers, not stock photos.
- **Proof**: testimonials with names, completed projects, numbers you can stand behind.
- **Clear contact details**: a phone number, an address, and a WhatsApp option.
- **Answers to worries before they ask**: pricing approach, documents, timelines, guarantees. A short FAQ section does this well.

## Is enquiring quick and easy?

This is where many websites quietly lose people:

- **Too many form fields.** Every extra field loses some visitors. Ask for the minimum you need to call back, usually a name and a mobile number, and make everything else optional. My own enquiry form asks for exactly that.
- **The form is hidden.** Put an "Enquire" button on every page, not only on the contact page.
- **No WhatsApp.** Many people would rather send a message than fill in a form or make a call.
- **No confirmation.** After submitting, people should see a clear "Thank you, we'll call you soon", so they don't submit twice or give up.

## Are enquiries being answered quickly?

Sometimes the website is working, and the enquiries are lost **after** they arrive: in an inbox nobody checks, or a WhatsApp group where nobody knows who is calling whom.

An enquiry is warmest in the first few minutes. Make sure:

- You get an **instant alert** when someone enquires.
- Every enquiry has **one person responsible** for it.
- You can see the **status** of every enquiry.

I wrote a whole post on this: [how to stop losing enquiries from your website](/blog/stop-losing-website-enquiries).

## How do you get more of the right visitors?

If traffic is the problem:

1. **Make sure Google can read your site.** Your text should be in the page itself, not only in images or loaded by scripts. I explain how to check this in [why ChatGPT couldn't read my portfolio](/blog/why-ai-assistants-could-not-read-my-portfolio).
2. **Have a page for each thing you sell or each place you serve.** One page that tries to rank for everything usually ranks for nothing.
3. **Set up your Google Business Profile** if customers come to you or you serve a local area. See [how to show up on Google when local customers search](/blog/show-up-on-google-local-customers).
4. **Answer the questions customers ask** in your own words, on your site. That is exactly what Google and AI assistants look for.

## A quick checklist

- [ ] You know your monthly visitors and enquiries
- [ ] The first screen says what you offer, for whom, and what to do next
- [ ] The site is fast and easy to use on a phone
- [ ] Real photos, proof and clear contact details
- [ ] A short enquiry form and a WhatsApp option on every page
- [ ] An instant alert and one owner for every enquiry
- [ ] A page for each product, service or location
